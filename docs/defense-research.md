# Thesis defense best practices — research notes (sourced, 2024–2026)

Facts with sources, gathered for a 20-minute PowerPoint soutenance de mémoire in a
francophone (France/Madagascar) academic context. Complements `docs/methodology.fr.md`.

---

## 1. Soutenance de mémoire — structure, jury criteria, mistakes

### 1.1 Format and duration (France)

- Master's defense in France: **15–20 min presentation + 20–30 min questions**, jury of
  **2–4 members** (directeur de mémoire, 1–2 external readers, sometimes a professional).
  M2 recherche: 20–30 min / 3–4 members; Master pro: 15–20 min / 2–3 members
  (table sourced from MESR-DGESIP EESR18 data): https://tesify.fr/soutenance-memoire-2026-powerpoint-plan-oral-questions-jury
- Adobe's guide: total soutenance 20–30 min; oral of ~15–20 min structured around
  contexte, problématique, méthodologie, résultats, conclusion:
  https://www.adobe.com/fr/acrobat/resources/academic-writing/master-thesis-defense.html
- Exposé length varies by institution, generally 10–30 min (Scribbr):
  https://www.scribbr.fr/memoire/la-soutenance-de-votre-memoire/
- **Check your institution's règlement and grading grid (grille d'évaluation) first** —
  rules differ per establishment (authorized supports, sitting/standing, mandatory
  PowerPoint, number of slides). Scribbr: same URL above.

### 1.2 What juries actually grade

Five evaluation criteria (FranceFG, guide 2026):
1. Maîtrise du sujet (do you really understand what you wrote?)
2. Structure de la présentation (logical progression)
3. Capacité d'argumentation (defending hypotheses with rigor)
4. Qualité de la prise de parole (clarity, audibility)
5. Gestion des questions (handling unforeseen questions)
Source: https://francefg.fr/powerpoint/soutenance-powerpoint/

Scribbr's list of jury axes: choix du sujet; problématique et questionnements; moyens de
recherche; réponses apportées; ouvertures; apports du travail. "Le jury notera avant
tout votre esprit de synthèse et votre capacité à expliquer un sujet complexe tout en
restant clair." https://www.scribbr.fr/memoire/la-soutenance-de-votre-memoire/

For a **soutenance de stage** specifically, graded items include: aptitude to
communicate in public, esprit de synthèse, maîtrise du sujet, compétences
professionnelles; example grid lines include "gestion du temps", "expression orale",
"conception (plan) et contenu", "réponses aux questions", "rigueur de l'analyse",
"capacité à mettre en valeur les points importants":
https://www.scribbr.fr/rapport-de-stage/soutenance-stage/

Key framing: **the jury has read the mémoire**. The soutenance is not an exhaustive
summary but a mise en perspective — highlight apports and demonstrate intellectual
maturity. https://tesify.fr/soutenance-memoire-2026-powerpoint-plan-oral-questions-jury

### 1.3 Best structure for a 20-minute presentation

Minute-by-minute plan for 18 min (Tesify, 2026):
- 0:00–2:30 accroche + contexte (never open with "Je vais vous parler de…")
- 2:30–5:30 problématique + ancrage théorique (3–5 key authors)
- 5:30–9:00 méthodologie (design, terrain, justification; announce limits yourself)
- 9:00–13:30 résultats principaux (3–4 salient results, visuals)
- 13:30–16:30 discussion et apports (confront results to hypotheses/literature)
- 16:30–18:00 limites, perspectives, strong closing sentence
Source: https://tesify.fr/soutenance-memoire-2026-powerpoint-plan-oral-questions-jury

7-part structure (FranceFG): page de garde + plan (1–2 min) → introduction/problématique
(2–3 min) → méthodologie (2–3 min) → **résultats et analyse (5–10 min, ~50% of time,
"le cœur de votre soutenance")** → discussion/apports (2–3 min) → limites et
perspectives (1–2 min, "OBLIGATOIRE et souvent négligée") → conclusion/remerciements
(1 min). https://francefg.fr/powerpoint/soutenance-powerpoint/

Scribbr's 20-min plan: Introduction 2–3 min (accroche, définition of key terms,
problématique) / Développement 15 min (méthodologie, objectifs, réponses, résultats) /
Conclusion 2–3 min (réponse à la problématique, limites, ouverture).
https://www.scribbr.fr/memoire/la-soutenance-de-votre-memoire/

Scribbr's 5-part PowerPoint plan: 1) choix du sujet; 2) méthodologie; 3) retour sur
les hypothèses; 4) conclusions; 5) apports. Number your slides (jury references them
during questions). https://www.scribbr.fr/memoire/exemple-powerpoint-soutenance/

**Results vs method order/time**: standard order is problème → objectif → méthode →
résultats → conclusions (Prezentacje AI: "Respectez l'ordre logique"). Allocate ~50%
of speaking time to results/analysis; method gets 2–3 min max; theory gets max 1–2
slides ("Le jury n'a pas besoin d'un cours sur des définitions qu'il connaît par
cœur"). https://prezentacje-ai.pl/fr/blog/presentation-soutenance-these and
https://francefg.fr/powerpoint/soutenance-powerpoint/

### 1.4 Slide count for 20 minutes

- FranceFG FAQ: **~1 slide per minute → 15–18 slides for 20 min** (title, plan,
  conclusion, remerciements included).
- Tesify FAQ: 15–18 slides, never fewer than 12 nor more than 20 for 20 min.
- Prezentacje AI: master = **14–18 slides for 15–20 min**; general rule 1–1.5 min per
  slide (title slide takes seconds; a results slide can take 2–3 min).
- Scribbr institution example: "PowerPoint obligatoire avec 3 diapos" for a
  10-min exposé — follow your consignes.

### 1.5 Common mistakes that lose points

The 7 fatal errors (Tesify): reading your slides; exceeding time ("le jury coupe la
parole sans hésitation — maîtrisez votre timing à 30 secondes près"); ignoring your
limits (denying them = "perte de crédibilité immédiate"); overloaded slides (one
slide = one idea, comprehensible in 5 seconds); not looking at the jury (eye contact
with every member, not only your directeur); apologizing for your work ("c'est
peut-être insuffisant mais…"); citing references you haven't read.
https://tesify.fr/soutenance-memoire-2026-powerpoint-plan-oral-questions-jury

The 7 recurring errors seen by juries (Prezentacje AI): walls of text (max 5–6 short
points per slide); reading slides instead of talking; bad structure (illogical jumps,
results before problem); ignoring the time limit (rehearse with a stopwatch); overly
long theoretical intro; illegible charts/tables (labels, legend, readable from the
back row); no backup copy (keep presentation in 3 places: USB stick, cloud, PDF in
email). https://prezentacje-ai.pl/fr/blog/presentation-soutenance-these

Time overrun costs: "Dépasser de 5 minutes peut suffire à vous faire perdre 2–3
points sur la note finale" (FranceFG). Exceeding time is "mal perçu" everywhere.

### 1.6 Speaker notes / orateur technique for non-expert presenters

- **Presenter mode** (mode présentateur): shows current + next slide, your notes,
  elapsed timer. Windows: F5; you see notes, jury doesn't.
  https://francefg.fr/powerpoint/soutenance-powerpoint/
- Don't memorize everything — speak with your own words using slides as
  aide-mémoire; know **intro (first 2 min) and conclusion (last minute) by heart** —
  those are the moments of direct eye contact (FranceFG).
- Notes on paper are allowed as aide-mémoire (transitions, exact figures) — but never
  read them continuously; "Un étudiant qui parle en regardant le jury convaincra
  toujours mieux que celui qui lit un texte préparé" (Tesify FAQ).
- Look at notes max 3 seconds ("règle des 3 secondes"), then back to jury (FranceFG).
- Rehearse 3–5 times aloud, standing, stopwatch in hand; first rehearsal typically
  runs 30% over time — cut ruthlessly; record yourself on phone to check débit,
  eye contact, screen-reading habit (FranceFG + Prezentacje AI).
- Plan to fill only **80–85% of allotted time** (stress distorts speaking speed)
  (Prezentacje AI).
- Identify 2–3 "secondary" slides you can skip if running late (FranceFG).
- Convention française: thank the jury **at the end**, briefly, before questions —
  not at the beginning ("Commencer par des remerciements prolongés est perçu comme
  une perte de temps") (Tesify FAQ).
- Stress: cognitive reappraisal ("I am excited" vs "I am stressed" — Jamieson/Harvard
  study, +17% oral performance claimed); body scan 2 min before entering; prepare a
  stock phrase for blanks: "Je souhaite revenir à cette question dans un instant si
  vous le permettez" (Tesify).

### 1.7 Handling jury questions

- Q&A phase often lasts as long as the exposé; in many schools a significant share of
  the grade sits here (FranceFG).
- Prepare 15–20 probable questions with ≤1-min answers; categories: méthodologiques,
  limites, compréhension ("précisez le concept X"), critiques ("n'auriez-vous pas dû
  considérer Y ?"), suite/perspectives (FranceFG).
- 30 typical jury questions listed (methodology, theoretical framework, results,
  limits, recul critique) — e.g. "Pourquoi cette méthode ?", "Votre échantillon
  est-il représentatif ?", "Quelles sont les trois principales limites ?":
  https://tesify.fr/soutenance-memoire-2026-powerpoint-plan-oral-questions-jury
- **Backup slides (annexes)**: 5–10 extra slides after the last slide with detailed
  stats, transcriptions, methodological schemas — jump to them when a pointed
  question arrives (FranceFG).
- If you don't know: admit honestly and offer a reasoned hypothesis. "Reconnaître
  honnêtement ses limites est mille fois mieux que d'inventer une réponse fragile."
  Same message in Tesify FAQ and Scribbr ("Etre honnête : si vous ne connaissez pas
  la réponse à certaines questions dites-le").

## 2. Rédaction du mémoire — French academic norms

### 2.1 Standard structure (pages préliminaires → annexes)

Full French mémoire structure (Scribbr, category page):
**Pages préliminaires**: couverture → page de garde → (éventuellement avant-propos,
préface) → remerciements → résumé → sommaire → liste des tableaux/figures →
**Corps**: introduction (sujet, problématique, annonce de plan) → partie théorique
(cadre théorique) → partie empirique (méthodologie + résultats) → conclusion et
discussion (réponse à la problématique, limites, ouverture) → (recommandations) →
**Pages finales**: bibliographie → annexes.
Source: https://www.scribbr.fr/category/memoire/ and
https://www.scribbr.fr/memoire/avant-la-redaction-dun-memoire/

Scribbr's précis on the conclusion: "on reprend l'essentiel, on répond à la
problématique et on ouvre le sujet" —
https://www.scribbr.fr/memoire/rediger-la-conclusion-et-la-discussion-de-votre-memoire/

### 2.2 Formatting conventions (mise en page) — Scribbr

https://www.scribbr.fr/elements-linguistiques/regles-mise-en-page/
- Police: **Times New Roman 12, interligne 1,5** (or double); acceptable
  alternatives Arial 11 or Calibri 12.
- Text **justifié**; paragraph starts with alinéa/indentation 0,5–1 cm.
- Footnotes smaller than body: e.g. Times 10 for Times 12 body.
- **Same police throughout, including titles.**
- Recto-only printing recommended.
- Margins: 2,54 cm all around (haut/bas/gauche/droite).
- Each chapter/grande partie starts on a **new page**.
- Page numbering: page de garde unnumbered; pages before the introduction numbered
  in **Roman numerals (II, III, …)**; Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3) from the
  introduction. Numbers reported in the sommaire.
- Page de garde must show: lieu des études, année universitaire, niveau d'études,
  titre du mémoire, your nom/prénom and the directeur's.
- Citations: entre **guillemets** (not italics), sources in footnotes; all works
  cited in the bibliographie.

### 2.3 Style — what makes French academic writing "human"

- **Paragraph method** (Scribbr, https://www.scribbr.fr/elements-linguistiques/comment-structurer-un-paragraphe/):
  1. one main idea per paragraph, 2. introduce/contextualize/define, 3. prove with
  an example (statistic, citation with author), 4. explain how the evidence supports
  the idea, 5. conclude the paragraph + transition to the next.
  Minimum ~5 sentences; not much longer than 3/4 of a page; avoid the purely
  énumératif paragraph.
- **Connecteurs / phrases de transition**: "Tout d'abord… Puis… Ensuite… Enfin…"
  inside paragraphs; dedicated transitions between sous-parties (same Scribbr
  article; see also their connecteurs logiques guide:
  https://www.scribbr.fr/elements-linguistiques/connecteurs-logiques/).
- Scribbr's style académique series covers: emploi de la 1ère personne (impersonal
  constructions preferred in French academic prose; "nous" de modestie standard),
  éviter les répétitions, mots interdits (registre familier, intensifs creux),
  registre à éviter, emploi des temps (imparfait/passé composé conventions per
  section), chiffres vs mots:
  https://www.scribbr.fr/elements-linguistiques/style-academique/
- Anti-"AI-sounding" writing, practical consensus from the above guides: vary
  sentence length and connecteurs (not just "De plus… De plus… En outre…"), keep
  impersonal/tournures nominatives where French expects them, ground every claim in a
  cited source or your own data (AI-sounding text is typically evidence-free and
  uniformly rhythmic), avoid formulaic openings ("Il est important de noter…",
  "Dans un monde de plus en plus…"), and keep one argued idea per paragraph with
  transitions that carry logic rather than decoration.
- Faire relire/corriger le mémoire et le PowerPoint avant remise — faults in the
  slides "pénaliseront fortement" at the soutenance (Scribbr, both soutenance
  articles).

### 2.4 Figures, tables, captions (légendes) in French theses

- Figures/graphs and tables must carry **légende + source**; schémas conceptuels
  "maison avec légende et source" are preferred over generic photos (Tesify:
  https://tesify.fr/soutenance-memoire-2026-powerpoint-plan-oral-questions-jury).
- In French academic convention (consistent across university guides): **table
  titles/titres go ABOVE the table; figure légendes go BELOW the figure**; separate
  numbering sequences ("Tableau 1, Tableau 2…" vs "Figure 1, Figure 2…"), with
  lists of tables and figures in the pages préliminaires (Scribbr structure:
  https://www.scribbr.fr/category/memoire/). Reference each in the text ("voir
  Tableau 3" — the local mémoire already follows this, cf. `docs/methodology.fr.md`
  §3 referencing "Tableau 3", "Tableau 7").
- In the body: keep raw/detailed tables for the **annexes**; the corps carries
  synthesized tables and charts (Scribbr annexes guide:
  https://www.scribbr.fr/memoire/annexes-memoire/; Prezentacje AI: no raw-data
  tables on slides).
- On slides: every chart readable from the back row — labeled axes, legend, one
  visualization per slide, highlight the most important element by color
  (Prezentacje AI: https://prezentacje-ai.pl/fr/blog/presentation-soutenance-these).

### 2.5 Mémoire de stage (internship) vs pure research mémoire

Soutenance de stage differs from a soutenance de recherche (Scribbr,
https://www.scribbr.fr/rapport-de-stage/soutenance-stage/):
- Purpose: demonstrate you integrated, understood, and can use the formation's
  teachings in a practical company environment.
- Split: **présentation = 2/3 of time, questions = 1/3**.
- Introduction must cover: yourself (nom, classe), the **entreprise** (nom, secteur,
  lieu, taille, organisation), your **maître de stage** (métier, parcours), your
  démarche to find the stage, then problématique + annonce de plan.
- Développement: what you DID — missions, analyses, données et méthodologie,
  résultats obtenus, préconisations. "Ne faites pas un résumé du rapport de stage…
  mieux vaut apporter des informations complémentaires."
- Conclusion: bilan critique (points positifs/négatifs, honest), apports
  professionnels et personnels, orientation professionnelle.
- Jury: usually 2–3 (responsable pédagogique, tuteur de stage, professeur).
- The mémoire de stage en entreprise still needs a problématique and a
  methodological treatment of the company project — the academic skeleton
  (problématique/hypothèses/méthode/résultats) stays, but "résultats" become the
  delivered work + recommandations, and a recommandations section for the host
  organization is standard (Scribbr: https://www.scribbr.fr/plan-memoire/les-recommandations-dans-un-memoire/).

## 3. PowerPoint design for academic defense

### 3.1 Text density and layout rules

- **Règle 6/6/6**: max 6 lines/slide, 6 words/line, 6 slides per idea block; >50
  words on a slide = rework it (FranceFG).
- **80/20 visual/text ratio**: 80% visual (chart, schema, photo, big number), 20%
  text max (FranceFG).
- Tesify: "si votre slide contient plus de 30 mots, elle est trop chargée"; one
  slide = one idea, understandable in 5 seconds.
- Prezentacje AI: max 5–6 points per slide, each a short phrase not a full sentence;
  details are given orally.
- Title rule: a slide title should communicate the **conclusion**, not the topic —
  "78 % des répondants préfèrent X" instead of "Résultats de la question 1"
  (Prezentacje AI, slide-model section).

### 3.2 Font sizes

| Element | Size | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Slide titles | 36–44 pt gras (Calibri/Arial/Helvetica Neue) | FranceFG |
| Body text | 24–28 pt | FranceFG |
| Minimum body | 24 pt (Calibri/Arial) | Tesify, Prezentacje AI |
| Titles (alt. source) | 36 pt | Prezentacje AI |
| Légendes/sources | 16–18 pt minimum, never below | FranceFG |
| Kawasaki's floor | 30 pt (business context) | see §3.4 |

- Sans-serif fonts: Inter, Calibri, Arial, Roboto; max **two font families** in the
  whole deck (Prezentacje AI).
- Number every slide — the jury refers to slide numbers during questions (Tesify).

### 3.3 Colors, backgrounds, animations

- Palette: **3 colors max** — dominante 60% / secondaire 30% / accent 10%; sobriety
  for academic context (bleu nuit + gris + accent neutre); avoid bright red/orange
  (FranceFG).
- Tesify: two colors max, light background for readability in a lit room.
- Background: plain, light (white/light gray) — dark backgrounds read poorly in
  well-lit amphitheaters (Prezentacje AI).
- Animations: quasi-invisible only — "Estomper" (fade) or "Apparaître" in; "Pulser"
  for one key figure per slide max. Banned: Vol plané, Toupie, Yo-yo, Boomerang,
  Rebond, Pivoter — gadget animations "font baisser la note mécaniquement" and
  signal weak content. One transition type for the whole deck (Fondu or Aucune).
  (FranceFG; Prezentacje AI concurs: no flying text, no sound effects.)
- If the university provides an official template with logo, use it (Prezentacje AI
  FAQ).

### 3.4 Guy Kawasaki 10/20/30 rule and its academic adaptations

Original source (Kawasaki, Dec 30, 2005, "The 10/20/30 Rule of PowerPoint"):
"a PowerPoint presentation should have **ten slides**, last no more than **twenty
minutes**, and contain **no font smaller than thirty points**."
- 10 slides because "a normal human being cannot comprehend more than ten concepts
  in a meeting"; his 10 VC topics: problem, solution, business model, underlying
  magic/technology, marketing and sales, competition, team, projections and
  milestones, status and timeline, summary and call to action.
- 30-pt floor rationale: small font = you don't know your material or think more
  text convinces; audience reads ahead of the speaker and desynchronizes. Fallback
  algorithm: "find out the age of the oldest person in your audience and divide it
  by two. That's your optimal font size."
- He explicitly states the rule is "applicable for any presentation to reach
  agreement" beyond VC pitches.
Archived original: http://web.archive.org/web/20130125060622/http://blog.guykawasaki.com/2005/12/the_102030_rule.html

**Academic adaptations** (converging French sources): the 20-minute element maps
directly onto a soutenance; the 10-slide element is relaxed for master's defenses —
14–18 slides is the accepted range because an academic defense must cover
problématique/méthodologie/résultats/limites in a fixed order (Prezentacje AI and
Tesify, §1.4 above); the 30-pt floor is relaxed to **24 pt minimum** for body text
(36 pt titles) because academic slides carry labeled data visualizations rather
than pitch text — but the principle stands: the smallest font on any slide
(légendes, sources, axis labels) must stay legible from the back of the room
(16–18 pt floor per FranceFG).

### 3.5 Presenting technical metrics to a mixed jury

- The jury has read the mémoire and includes non-specialists or externals: assume "un
  regard complètement extérieur" and expect explanations that stay clear on a
  complex subject (Scribbr). Esprit de synthèse is the explicitly graded criterion.
- Isolate **chiffres clés in large format**; use avant/après comparisons; frame
  verbatims; "pas de tableaux Excel surchargés" (FranceFG, results-section rules).
- Reserve technical details for the mémoire's annexes and for **backup slides**;
  in the oral, keep l'essentiel (FranceFG).
- Each results slide carries one finding, title states the finding (Prezentacje AI).
- One chart per slide; label axes; legend; color-highlight the one element that
  matters (Prezentacje AI).
- For engineering/project work specifically: extra slides for concrete
  demonstrations — screenshots, system schemas, performance test results —
  "les jurys techniques apprérient les éléments concrets plus que les jolis
  graphiques" (Prezentacje AI, licence/ingénieur section).

### 3.6 Data visualization on slides — chart rules

- Etiquettes lisibles, légende, description; mise en évidence par la couleur of the
  most important element; **une seule visualisation par diapositive** (Prezentacje
  AI design rules).
- Chart legibility from the last row is a listed grading-killer if violated
  (Prezentacje AI error #6).
- Prefer clear graphs over heavy tables; large isolated key numbers; the jury reads
  the mémoire for detail — slides carry synthesis (FranceFG + Tesify).
- Self-made conceptual schemas with légende + source > generic stock images
  (Tesify).

### 3.7 Day-J logistics (convergent checklists)

- Export the deck **to PDF as backup**; carry it on USB + cloud + emailed to
  yourself; test on the room's machine the day before if possible (FranceFG,
  Prezentacje AI, Tesify J-7/J-1 checklists).
- Rehearse the full deck 2–5 times with a stopwatch; final full rehearsal the day
  before, not the morning of (FranceFG, Tesify).
- Submit the final deck to your directeur for relecture ~1 week before (Tesify J-7).
- Arrive 20–30 min early; water at the pupitre; 7–8 h sleep (Tesify, FranceFG).
